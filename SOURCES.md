# Mainline release-order notes

Verified November 18, 2026. Dates refer to the earliest release in any region. The guide list includes official mainline originals, remakes, and remasters. Ports, spin-offs, compilations, and unofficial games are excluded. Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom each group their Nintendo Switch 2 Edition with the original game; their guide entries retain the original 2017 and 2023 dates.

- [Nintendo Japanese series history](https://www.nintendo.com/jp/character/zelda/history/index.html?lang=ja) supplies the original Japanese launch dates, including the original game, Zelda II, A Link to the Past, Link’s Awakening, Ocarina of Time, Majora’s Mask, and the Oracle games.
- [Nintendo US series history](https://www.nintendo.com/us/explore/characters/zelda/history/) supplies later launch dates and confirms the 2026 Ocarina of Time remake. Earlier Japanese or European releases are used where applicable. Nintendo gives December 2, 2002 for the cartridge containing the original Four Swords; that original adventure remains in the list, while the bundled A Link to the Past port is excluded.
- [Nintendo October 2013 IR release table](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2013/131031e.pdf) establishes The Wind Waker HD’s September 20, 2013 North American digital launch, before its Japanese or North American physical releases.
- [Nintendo August 2025 IR release table](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/ir/pdf/2025/250801_2e.pdf?pubDate=20250802) confirms June 5, 2025 for both Nintendo Switch 2 Editions, which are grouped under their original game entries.
- [Nintendo’s Ocarina of Time launch announcement](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/its-time-experience-the-legend-of-zelda-ocarina-of-time-reborn-exclusively-for-nintendo-switch-2/) confirms November 5, 2026 and describes the Nintendo Switch 2 version as a remake built from the ground up.
- [The consolidated Zelda media catalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_The_Legend_of_Zelda_media) cross-checks the mainline originals, 3D remakes, and HD remasters.

Platform suffixes distinguish same-named remakes in the guide list and are not asserted subtitles on the packaging. Twilight Princess combines its original launch platforms. Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom list all covered consoles in their grouped entries.

## Artwork

The banner uses a published Master Sword illustration © Nintendo, reproduced from *The Legend of Zelda Encyclopedia*, Dark Horse Books, p. 167, via [Zelda Wiki’s artwork record](https://zeldawiki.wiki/wiki/File:BotW_Master_Sword_Artwork.png). The [original transparent image](https://cdn.wikimg.net/en/zeldawiki/images/e/eb/BotW_Master_Sword_Artwork.png) was uploaded in 2019; its background was removed in 2020. The sword is published game artwork, not a newly generated interpretation. It is reproduced without redrawing the sword. The surrounding typography, borders, and status icons are editable vector graphics.
